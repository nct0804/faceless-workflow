# Day One Films: Style Bible

A study of the channel's latest 20 uploads (Oct–Nov 2026). Every video was processed frame by frame at 30 fps for cuts, motion, camera movement, brightness, contrast and color, plus audio tempo and cut timing. On-screen text was read by OCR every 0.5 s, and spoken audio comes from captions. The raw numbers are in `stats.csv`, and the full scripts are in `transcripts/`.

---

## 0. The one-sentence formula

> **A blank, faceless "everyman" tries to beat one bad habit. A number on screen tracks his decline, and a shadow version of himself (the inner voice) talks him down in red text. A one-line realization flips the story, a DAY-counter montage shows the slow climb back, and two short lines of moral close it out over sad piano.**

Every one of the 18 short films follows that formula. The two "explainer" videos (#5 and #6) use the same visual world with a voiceover added.

| Format | Count | Length | Voice | Examples |

**Takeaways:**
- He doesn't need a voice. The silent shorts are the main product and the breakout hits. Silence also makes the videos language-light and globally watchable.
- Topics are a narrow lane of self-improvement aimed at teen and young-adult men: lust/porn, phone, gaming, discipline vs. motivation, comparison, comfort zone, overthinking, loneliness, self-respect, perfectionism, bullying, addiction (pills), time with parents.
- Seasonal hooks: "No Nut November" (lust videos in Oct/Nov), "Winter Arc."
- Titles take one of two shapes: **"[Thing] Is Killing You."** / **"You're Not X."** / **"[Phrase]."** (always ending in a period), or **"WORD | An Animated Short Film"**. The period makes the title read like a verdict.

## 2. The script: how every short is written

### 2.1 The three voices (all on-screen text, no audio)

| Voice | Look | Grammar | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Narrator** | White, sentence case, small rounded "comic"-style font, centered at the **bottom** (subtitle position) or top. Sometimes typed on letter by letter | Third person, past tense ("He…"), **median 5 words, max 9**, always ends with a period | Moves the story along. Never explains a feeling the picture already shows |
| **Inner critic** | **Red** (#C34433), lowercase, placed beside the shadow figure | Second person, present tense ("you'll mess it up.", "you deserve it.", "nobody wants you.") | The antagonist. Its lines repeat across the film and get quieter (or the hero answers "no.") |
| **Dialogue** | Small white lowercase text in dark speech pills, or phone chat bubbles | Real texting language: "u up?", "ball tomorrow?", "can't.", "lol ok" | Shows the cost: friends asking, family inviting, then the asking stops |

Reading time: **about 0.6 s per word** on screen (a 5-word line stays up about 3 s). Lines appear on cuts or on musical hits.

### 2.2 The beat sheet (measured across 18 shorts, typical timestamps for a ~3:20 film)

| # | Beat | Time | What happens | Real examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold-open premise line** | 0:00–0:03 | One narrator sentence over the first image. It's either a quiet origin or an "everyone has X" statement | "It started with just one game." / "It started with just one video." / "Everyone had a number." / "He thought he had time." / "He could draw anything. He just never finished." / "It was just one word." |
| 2 | **Introduce the meter** | 0:03–0:20 | A HUD number or bar appears and becomes the visual spine of the film (see §4.1) | DAYS LEFT: 4,380 · SCREEN TIME TODAY 0h05m · TALKED: 1 / TRAINED: 0 · DAYS CLEAN: 3 · MOOD bar · STARTED: 1 / FINISHED: 0 · price tag $100 · "62 / YOU" |
| 3 | **The good life (brief)** | 0:05–0:30 | Friends, sport, family, a hint of who he could be. Warm light | "He had friends." "He had confidence." "He used to go everywhere with his friends." |
| 4 | **First taste / first wound** | 0:20–0:40 | The habit begins, or the insult lands. It feels good or it hurts. **The shadow critic appears** | "It felt good." / "yo. look at him." "bro's so ugly." |
| 5 | **Escalation montage** | 0:40–1:30 | Fast cuts with a timestamp card (WEEK 1… / DAY 3… / NEXT DAY) and the meter getting worse. The habit costs relationships: invites declined ("can't. gotta go."), family ignored ("GET OUT.", "nah."), friends stop asking | "He stopped going out." / "His friends stopped asking." / "He wasn't having fun anymore. But he couldn't stop." |
| 6 | **Rock bottom** | ~1:30–1:55 (≈ 45–55%) | A quiet, nearly black, long-held shot. Critic at its biggest. Often a red-flooded frame or a stat reveal | "It never gave him anything. It only took." / "7 HOURS A DAY = 106 DAYS A YEAR = 17 YEARS OF HIS LIFE." |
| 7 | **The flip: two-line realization** | ~1:55–2:10 (≈ 55–60%) | A short narrator couplet that reframes the problem, usually **"X. / Y."** with a reversal | "Nobody lowered his price. / He did." · "They said it once. / He said it every day." · "Broken isn't finished. / It's where the gold goes." · "It was never his time. It was theirs." · "He'd drawn every line himself." |
| 8 | **Recovery montage with a DAY counter** | 2:10–2:50 | Fast-cut counter (DAY 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 14, 30 / NIGHT 1, 2, 3, 15, 30). Meter improving. Critic returns with the same lines and the hero ignores it. Friends come back ("yo! you came!") | "The first days were the hardest." / "Every day, it got a little easier." / "Every urge felt like forever. But every urge passed." |
| 9 | **Final test** | ~2:50–3:05 | The critic makes one last full-strength attack (tryouts, final exam, skate jam, "just once."). He answers with an action or a one-word "no." | "you'll fall." "they'll laugh." "go home." → he drops in |
| 10 | **Moral couplet** | last ~15 s before CTA | Two short lines, the second a command or a reversal. Often echoes the title | "The game was never the problem. / Losing himself was." · "Play the game. / Don't let it play you." · "Move in silence. / Let your success make the noise." · "Your only competition / is who you were yesterday." · "You're not broken. / You're just not finished yet." · "Real life was slower. / But it was real. / Choose real." |
| 11 | **CTA** | final 8–15 s | "If this hit you, like & subscribe." then "Want to make videos like this? Join the Day One Films community on Skool." (the business model) | — |

**Script craft rules seen in all 18:**
- **A callback structure.** A line or image from the first 30 s comes back transformed at the end ("kick the ball?" / "later, dad." → years later "dad. play?" / "always."). The opening object becomes the closing image (the trophy, the sketchbook, the plant, the price tag).
- **Rule of three, then break it.** "Every summer he said the same thing." Three snoozes. Three "ugly"s, then a hundred.
- **The critic's lines are the habit's real voice**, written like a manipulative friend: "no one will know.", "you deserve it.", "you'll stop tomorrow.", "just once.", "you earned one.", "come back when you feel it."
- **Concrete over abstract.** Never "he became isolated." Always the text thread going quiet, or "WORDS SAID TODAY: 0."
- **Words used per film:** about 25–65 words of narration in total. If a picture can say it, no caption.
- **No villains except the habit.** Bullies forget ("He didn't even remember."), and parents are kind.

### 2.3 Narrated explainers (#5, #6) script pattern

- **Hook (0:00–0:30):** second-person, blunt. "Your brain hates discipline." / "Day one. You feel disgusted with yourself."
- **Promise and retention bait:** "Here are seven things… **And stay for number seven. It's the one your brain hates most.**"
- **One science paragraph** in plain words: "It runs on dopamine. Not the pleasure chemical, the one that makes you *want* things." Exactly one statistic per section, cited vaguely ("One small study…", "in one study, people in cold water had their dopamine rise by 250%").
- **Each step / day:** title card ("STEP 5. DO 20 PUSH-UPS." / "DAY 3") → the bad version acted out → "Now, try this." → a tip ("Tip, decide the night before.").
- **Delivery:** 100–107 wpm, phrases of 2–6 words separated by 0.5–1.5 s pauses (83 pauses over 1.5 s in 9 min). Lots of one-word lines: "Snooze, snooze, snooze." "It's dark. It's cold. Later."
- **Expectation management near the end:** "Now, the truth. You will miss a morning… The only rule: never miss twice."
- **Close:** recap transformation (Day 1 → Day 30, "same guy") → mirror the opening line → **comment CTA** ("If you're starting tomorrow, comment *day one*.").

## 3. Visual style

### 3.1 Characters
- **The hero:** a faceless-ish, bald, bean-shaped body in flat **cream (#E2DDD1)**. Thin dark outline, noodle arms and legs, no hands detail, dot eyes, and a single-line mouth and brows. All emotion is carried by **eyebrows + mouth + posture** (shoulders drop, head tilts down when low).
- **Other people:** the same body, recolored with skin tones (#BE906C and similar) plus **one identifying accessory**: a cap color (green / red / blue / yellow hard hat), a beanie, or hair. The little brother always wears a red beanie. Friends are a cap color you can read from across the frame.
- **The inner critic:** a **solid black silhouette** of the hero, slightly taller, with **glowing red slit eyes and a red rim glow / outer glow** around the whole body, sometimes with a red pool or ring on the floor. It stands beside or behind him, leans in to whisper, and grows larger (up to full-screen) at rock bottom. At the end it's small, faded, or stepped past. **This is the signature of the channel.**
- **Close-ups:** a mid-shot of the face, centered, with the background heavily **blurred (shallow depth of field / bokeh)**. Used for every emotional reaction beat.

### 3.2 World and composition
- **Side-on 2D "stage" sets**, like a diorama: bedroom (bed, nightstand with red LED clock, window with moon), classroom (chalkboard with "x + 4 = 10"), school lockers (rows of tall lockers), gym, street at night with lampposts, kitchen table under a pendant lamp, basketball court with chain fence, bus stop.
- Characters stand on a single **floor line about 1/4 from the bottom**. Frames are wide with lots of negative space (dark) above.
- **Recurring insert shots** (each appears in most videos):
  1. **POV phone in hands**: two cream hands holding a phone, chat app or notification, background blurred. Phone screens are the main way plot info arrives.
  2. **Top-down desk/table shot** on a **warm brown wood** surface: a test paper with a red-ink grade, a note "PROUD OF YOU :)" on a lunch tray, a sticky note, a calendar page.
  3. **Hand insert**: a hand writing, tapping the alarm, taking pills.
  4. **Big bold title card** on black ("DAY 3", "STEP 5.").
  5. **Diagram / infographic**: a line graph of a craving "wave", a testosterone peak, the "COMFORT ZONE" map blob that shrinks, the dopamine curve.
  6. **Grid repetition**: the same shot tiled 3×6 to say "hundreds of times."
- **Time is always labeled**: a small "DAY 4" / "WEEK 6" / "NEXT DAY" / "THAT NIGHT" / "ONE YEAR LATER" in a corner (bottom-right or top-left) or top center.

### 3.3 Lighting, shadow and contrast (measured)
- **Very dark.** Average frame brightness is **19–49 out of 255** (median ~33). About **70% of all pixels are near-black** (#010101 → #24252D). Blacks are true black (5th percentile = 0 in every video).
- **Highlights are low and controlled:** 95th percentile ~80–150. Nothing is ever blown out, and the brightest thing is the cream character or a lamp.
- **Motivated pools of light:** one **pendant lamp or streetlamp per scene**, a cone of warm light (soft radial gradient) falling on the character, plus a small glowing bulb dot. Window moonlight. Phone glow on the face at night.
- **Heavy vignette:** the center is much brighter than the edges in every video (vignette score 29–82). Edges fall into black.
- **Shadows:** very simple, a soft dark ellipse under the feet, no cast shadows. Depth comes from **the light pool + vignette + blur**, not from rendered shadows.
- **Color grading by emotion:**
  - Normal: desaturated cool charcoal-blue night (saturation 16–58 out of 255, low).
  - **Anxiety / critic attack:** the whole frame is **flooded red** (multiply or tint), text duplicates in red, the critic's glow intensifies.
  - **Hope / good memories:** warm amber light, daylight scenes with a green hill and blue sky (rare, so they pop).
  - **Explainer videos are the darkest** (brightness ~20, saturation ~16), basically black stage + single light.
- **Palette** (k-means over 300 frames):

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Background black | `#010101` `#0C0C0D` `#19171A` |
| Night blue-charcoal walls | `#24252D` `#474854` |
| Warm dark brown (wood, floor) | `#35221C` `#3C3432` `#65412F` |
| Hero cream | `#E2DDD1` / `#E5E0D3` |
| Skin tone (other people) | `#BE906C` |
| Critic red / red text / red ink | `#C34433` (glow brighter, around `#E0402A`) |
| Grey (neutral props) | `#939699` `#6D6866` |
| Accent (UI bars, sunrise) | amber/orange; blue for lockers/trays |

### 3.4 Texture and finishing
- Subtle **film grain / noise** over the frame (visible as low-level per-frame change in flat black areas).
- Glows on all light sources (bloom), the critic's eyes and red rim, and the hero when "motivated" (a yellow glow with floating sparks in *Motivation Will Fail You*).
- Thin, slightly sketchy **white outlines** on dark props so they read against black.
- Particles: snow, rain streaks, dust motes in light beams, floating "ugly" words.
- **Letterbox look:** black bands top and bottom come from the dark vignette rather than real bars.

## 4. Signature devices (the "idea engine")

### 4.1 The on-screen meter
Each film has **one number or bar that makes the theme measurable**, rendered as a minimal HUD:

| Video | Meter |
|---|---|
| Self Respect | Price tag $100 → $66 → $40 → FREE → "NOT FOR SALE" (crossed out with a red marker) |
| Comparison Trap | Circled score above every head ("62 YOU" vs "91 MAX") |
| You Don't Have Time | DAYS LEFT: 4,380 → … → DAYS LEFT WITH DAD: 9 |
| Your Phone Is Killing You | SCREEN TIME TODAY 0h05m → 9h17m |
| Move In Silence | TALKED: 47 / TRAINED: 0 → TRAINED: 180 |
| Lust Is Poison | DAYS CLEAN: 3 → 0 → 89 |
| Gaming / Winter Arc | MOOD bar above head |
| Loneliness | WORDS SAID TODAY: 0 |
| Perfectionism | STARTED: 214 / FINISHED: 0 |
| Motivation Will Fail You | MOTIVATION bar 34% → 0%, DAY counter |
| Roots | ABOVE GROUND 0 cm (a plant), the roots grow underground 8.0 m |
| One Last Time | FEEL bar (numbness) |
| Comfort Zone | A glowing map blob that shrinks and grows |

**When writing a new one, find the number first.** The meter turns an abstract feeling into a plot you can watch go up and down.

### 4.2 The shadow critic
A visible antagonist for an internal struggle. Its arc: appears early (whispering) → grows → full-screen at rock bottom → returns at the final test → is ignored or shrinks. The hero's win is never a fight, it's **not listening**.

### 4.3 The physical metaphor
One object that carries the theme: the price tag, the plant and its roots, kintsugi gold cracks ("Broken isn't finished. It's where the gold goes."), the comfort-zone line drawn on the floor ("It's just a line you drew. Step over it."), the marker writing "ugly" on himself ("The marker was in his hand.").

### 4.4 Phones as narrators
Chat threads, notifications, screen-time reports and YouTube thumbnails (parody motivational videos: "NO MORE EXCUSES - wake up speech") deliver plot without narration.

## 5. Editing and motion (measured at 30 fps)

### 5.1 Cutting
- **Hard cuts only.** No dissolves or crossfades were found in frame-by-frame checks. Transitions are a hard cut, a cut to black, or (rarely) a TV-static glitch.
- **Shot length:** silent shorts average **2.9–5 s per shot** (median of medians ~3.5 s). 21–85 cuts per film. The explainers are slower (7–8 s).
- **Rhythm changes with the story.** The opening and rock bottom have long holds (8–20 s, e.g. a 20-second hold on "DAYS LEFT WITH DAD: 9"). The montages are rapid-fire (0.5–1.5 s per DAY card).
- **Cuts follow the music:** 40–100% of cuts land within 0.1 s of a musical onset, versus ~30% expected by chance. In the strongest examples (*Loneliness* 100%, *Phone* 83%, *Broken* 75%, *Lust Is Poison* 75%), the piano note is the cut.

### 5.2 Camera
- **A constantly drifting camera.** Almost no shot is truly locked. There's a slow **push-in (scale ~0.4–0.9% per second, up to ~1.6%)** on most shots, so a 5 s shot goes from 100% → ~103%. Push-ins outnumber pull-outs about 5:3.
- Slow lateral **pans** across set pieces (the locker row, the street), usually in the direction of the character's walk.
- Occasional **tilt** to reveal (up from the soil to the sky in *Roots*, down to the plant).
- No handheld shake, whip pans, or fast zooms. Calm, observational, a "documentary of a small life."

### 5.3 Character animation
- **Smooth, tweened 2D puppet animation at the full 30 fps** (about 85–93% of frames are unique, no "on twos" holds). Body parts are rigged and moved with easing, not hand-drawn frame by frame.
- **Slow and minimal acting:** idle breathing, small head turns, slumped shoulders, slow walk cycles. Expression changes are simple brow and mouth swaps.
- Small loopable motions carry the scene: blinking, breathing, a phone thumb scrolling, rain falling, a light flickering.

## 6. Sound

- **Music: slow, emotional, sparse piano or ambient pads**, often lo-fi and reverb-heavy. Tempo-detection mostly lands around **90–110 BPM** (or its double/half). It's quiet under the opening and swells at the recovery and moral.
- **No voiceover** in the shorts. There's **sound design** for key actions instead: a phone buzz, an alarm, a door, a "haha" crowd laugh (captioned as text), heartbeat or monitor beep.
- **Silence as punctuation:** before the flip line, the music drops out or thins.
- Explainers have a calm, low, unhurried male VO, close-mic, with music ducked under it.

## 7. Packaging

- **Titles:** "[Habit] Is Killing You." · "You're Not [X]." · "[Command phrase]." · "WORD | An Animated Short Film". Short, declarative, period at the end.
- **Description:** 2–3 lines of the film's moral (the couplet), then a Skool CTA, then 4–5 hashtags (#discipline #motivation #selfimprovement #animation #shortfilm, plus seasonal ones like #nonutnovember).
- **Upload cadence:** about every 3–4 days.
- **Outlier analysis:** *Lust Is Killing You.* (2.78M) has the purest form of the formula: a universal and embarrassing private habit, a "you" title that feels like it's about the viewer, the friend group as the emotional cost, and a clear "Choose real." command. *DISCIPLINE* (6.4M, just outside this 20) is the same structure. Explainers and personal topics (parents, loneliness) perform lower than "[habit] is killing you."

## 8. Recipe: making a video with this grammar (without copying his look)

> Use the **grammar** (meter, shadow critic, three text voices, beat sheet, dark lighting, cut-on-beat, slow push-ins). **Change the surface** so you have your own channel and not a knockoff: your own character silhouette, your own critic design (not the black-silhouette-with-red-glow), your own accent color instead of red, your own font, your own meter visuals, your own music.

**Script (1 page, ~40–60 words of captions):**
1. Pick a habit and its cost (who stops asking?).
2. Pick the **meter** (what number tells the story?).
3. Pick the **physical metaphor object**.
4. Write the **flip couplet** and the **moral couplet** first, then build backward.
5. Write 4–6 inner-critic lines in the voice of a manipulative friend, and reuse them in act 3.
6. Fill the beat sheet in §2.2 with 35–70 shots.

**Look:**
- Background around 15–40 brightness out of 255, one warm light pool per scene, strong vignette, grain, bloom on lights.
- Character in one light, flat, matte color. Everyone else in mid-tones.
- One accent color reserved for the antagonist, red ink, and alarm. Nothing else uses it.

**Edit:**
- Hard cuts on piano notes. 3–4 s average shot. Montages at 0.5–1.5 s per card.
- Every shot gets a slow 2–4% push-in (or slow pan), eased.
- Captions: max ~9 words, 0.6 s per word on screen, bottom-center for the narrator.

**Tools (likely, not confirmed by the creator, whose exact workflow is sold in his Skool):** vector-drawn characters with rigged, tweened puppet animation (After Effects + Duik/puppet pins, Moho, Cavalry or Rive all fit), with glow, grain, vignette and blur done in the compositor.
